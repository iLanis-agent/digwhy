dig prints twenty lines. Operators read three.

DigWhy decodes real dig output the way an operator skims it: the status that matters, the flags that tell you whether the answer is authoritative or cached, and the difference between ANSWER: 0 that means "no such name" and ANSWER: 0 that means "no such record type".

NXDOMAIN is not NODATA

An empty answer with status NXDOMAIN means the name does not exist. An empty answer with status NOERROR and an SOA in AUTHORITY means the name exists but has no record of your type - a null MX like 0 . means something else again: "this domain accepts no email, by design" (RFC 7505).

The flags are the story

No aa? Your answer came from a resolver's cache, and the TTL is counting down, not fresh from the zone. tc? The answer was truncated - re-run over TCP. ad? The resolver DNSSEC-validated it.

CNAME chains, drawn out

Aliased names resolve hop by hop, and DigWhy draws the whole chain from the answer section so you can see where it lands - and where it breaks.
